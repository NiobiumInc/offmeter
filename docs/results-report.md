# Results Report: Demand-Response Eligibility under FHE
_Generated by the Niobium FHE Application Design AI Assistant (FHEanna)._ (v0.13.0)

## How we know it passes (for a non-cryptographer)

**(a) The answer key is a measured load impact.** Each held-out household's label
comes from simulating a real demand-response event and applying the settlement
arithmetic a utility would actually use: compute the Customer Baseline Load
10-in-10 style (the 5–9pm hours averaged over the 10 most recent similar
weekdays), subtract the actual event load, and call the household eligible if the
measured reduction clears a kW threshold. The answer key is
`data/test_labels.csv`, which carries the reduction in kW alongside the label.

**(b) Two different questions.** This report answers both, and keeps them apart:

- *Is the model any good?* Measured by scoring the plaintext reference model
  against those measured labels.
- *Does the encrypted pipeline compute what the model says?* Measured by
  comparing the encrypted output to the faithful twin, record by record.

**(c) The test.** Households are encrypted and scored *blind* by the server,
which never sees the consumption series or any of the six derived quantities. The
client decrypts each returned probability and compares it to the twin. Agreement
is exact at the decision level (0 flips) and the probabilities match to within a
few ten-millionths.

**(d) Why any difference exists at all.** Two small, budgeted-for effects. The
smooth sigmoid is replaced by a degree-13 polynomial stand-in (max 1.58e-4 in
probability, 0 decision flips), plus a faint hiss of CKKS encryption noise at
~4e-7 on top, a further two-and-a-half orders of magnitude smaller.

**(e) Consistent across four layers.** The same answer is reproduced by the
plaintext faithful twin, the encrypted CPU run, the ring-level FHETCH simulator
(**bit-identical** to the OpenFHE computation), and a **live run on the Niobium
Fog**.

**(f) Scope of these results.** The households and weather are **seeded
synthetic**, built to exercise the pipeline. The model-quality numbers are real
measurements against real held-out labels, but on a synthetic domain; predictive
quality on real consumers is a separate question that belongs to whoever trains
the production model. The model form is the CalTRACK/TOWT specification used in
regulated settlement.

### Reading the model-quality row

**The operating point.** A utility ranks households and recruits down the list to
a budget or capacity target, so quality is quoted at a recruitment depth rather
than a probability cut. Decile ranking is the reporting convention for targeting
models, and top-decile lift is the standard published benchmark (customer-targeting
work generally reports 2.0× to 3.5×). Precision here holds flat through the first
two deciles and falls from the third, so **top 20%** is the deepest depth that
still recruits at the full hit rate, capturing twice the eligibles of a top-10%
cut at identical precision.

| Recruitment depth | Recruited | Precision | Eligibles captured | Lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| top 10% | 40 | 0.600 | 22.4% | 2.24× |
| **top 20%** | **80** | **0.600** | **44.9%** | **2.24×** |
| top 30% | 120 | 0.567 | 63.6% | 2.12× |
| top 40% | 160 | 0.512 | 76.6% | 1.92× |
| top 50% | 200 | 0.475 | 88.8% | 1.78× |

**Read against the achievable ceiling.** The labels are noisy by construction,
because event response is driven by a behavioural `willingness` parameter that
never appears in metered load. A model with perfect knowledge of every *physical*
household parameter, but still blind to willingness, also reaches **ROC-AUC
0.839**. The six derived quantities therefore extract everything extractable from
the baseline period, and the remaining gap to perfect prediction is behavioural.
Because that ceiling is a property of the synthetic generator, these figures
demonstrate the pipeline rather than forecast real-world performance.

### Live Fog run

A **live Fog submission was performed** and passed. The default (no-flag) mode
dispatched the per-household server step under `fog submit --target=FOG`; the Fog
reconstructed the result from the trace and returned it, and the client decrypted
and checked it against the twin: max error **3.715e-7**, **0 flips**, **PASS**.
The Fog path is validated by execution.

### Caveat: the polynomial error is still the binding term near the boundary

The pass bar certifies **encryption** noise, which is comfortably small. The
**polynomial** error remains the larger term: max 1.579e-4 against a minimum twin
decision margin of 5.462e-5, and **1 of 400** households sits closer to the 0.5
threshold than the polynomial error. Measured twin-vs-reference agreement is 100%
(0 flips); for a household on the boundary that agreement is a measured outcome on
this seed.

The margin comes from matching the Chebyshev domain to the measured operand range,
[−5, 5], at the same degree and the same depth. Widening it to [−8, 8] costs
2.958e-3 of error and puts 2 of 400 households at risk.

## run_test default behavior (Fog)

`run_test.sh` with **no flag** targets the Niobium Fog and **never silently falls
back to the local simulator**:

- **No key** (`FOG_API_TOKEN` unset and no `~/.fog/credentials`) → prints the
  sign-in / sign-up pointer and exits 0.
- **Key present but not launched under `fog submit`** → prints how to dispatch.
- **Key present, under `fog submit`** → dispatches, as recorded above.
