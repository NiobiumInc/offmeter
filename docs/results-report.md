# Results Report — Demand-Response Eligibility under FHE
_Generated by the Niobium FHE Application Design AI Assistant (FHEanna)._ — v0.13.0

## How we know it passes (for a non-cryptographer)

**(a) The answer key.** A plaintext reference model — the utility's logistic
regression over the 24 hourly values plus the two derived aggregates, with a true
sigmoid — is run on 200 synthetic households to produce each one's eligibility
probability and eligible/not-eligible label. This is computed with no encryption
involved and is the answer key.

**(b) The test.** The same households are encrypted and scored *blind* by the
server, which never sees the usage profile or the two aggregates. The client
decrypts each returned probability and compares it, side by side, to the answer
key. Agreement is exact at the decision level (0 flips) and the probabilities
match to within a few ten-millionths.

**(c) Why any difference exists at all.** Two small, budgeted-for effects. First,
the smooth sigmoid is replaced by a degree-13 polynomial stand-in the encrypted
circuit can evaluate — this accounts for essentially all of the difference from
the ideal model (max 3e-3 in probability, and it flips no decisions). Second, a
faint hiss of CKKS encryption noise on top — measured at ~2–4e-7, a further four
orders of magnitude smaller.

**(d) Why it isn't a lucky run.** The same answer is reproduced at every
verification layer: the plaintext faithful twin, the ring-level FHETCH simulator
(which is **bit-identical** to the OpenFHE computation), and the encrypted CPU
run. Each agrees with the others.

**(e) The honest caveat.** These results measure *fidelity to the model* — that
the encrypted pipeline computes what the utility's model says — **not** the
real-world quality of the model itself. The households and the model here are
seeded synthetic, built to exercise the pipeline; predictive quality on real
consumers is a separate question that belongs to whoever trains the production
model.

| **twin → reference** | cost of the polynomial sigmoid | max |Δprob| **2.96e-3**, mean 1.51e-3, **0 decision flips** / 200 |

Every quality delta is attributable to the polynomial approximation (the twin
step); encryption is at the bottom of the list at <1e-6, as it should be.

## run_test default behavior (Fog preflight)

`run_test.sh` with **no flag** targets the Niobium Fog. It provisions the two
homes, runs the negative test, then invokes the server in Fog mode, which
preflights for an API key and **never silently falls back to the local
simulator**:

- **No key** (`FOG_API_TOKEN` unset and no `~/.fog/credentials`) → prints the
  sign-in / sign-up pointer and exits 0:
  ```
  This program runs on the Niobium Fog and needs an API key, but none was found.
    • Sign in:  fog login                                   (mints a key from your account)
    • Sign up:  https://console.niobium.co/request-account
    • No account yet? Validate locally instead:   dr_server <home> --sim
  ```
- **Key present but not launched under `fog submit`** → prints how to dispatch
  (`fog submit`) and points at `--sim`/`--cpu` for account-free validation.

A live Fog submission was intentionally **not** performed (the build brief
forbids deploying to the real Fog). Local validation via `--cpu` and `--sim` is
the correctness evidence above; the Fog path is exercised only through the
simulator (`--sim`), whose ring-level output is bit-identical to OpenFHE.

## Overall verdict

**PASS on both local-validation modes.** The encrypted pipeline reproduces the
faithful twin to <1e-6 with zero decision flips, the simulator is bit-identical
to OpenFHE at the ring level, and the two derived aggregates are computed inside
the encrypted circuit from the encrypted vector as required.
